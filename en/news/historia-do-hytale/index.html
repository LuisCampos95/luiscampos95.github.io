Hytale: the full saga, from the 2018 trailer to Update 7

From the Hypixel server to Update 7: the record trailer, the Riot buyout, the engine switch, the cancellation and the buyback of Hytale, with dates.

Few games have reached players by such a crooked road. Between the start of the project in 2015 and Early Access on January 13, 2026, Hytale got a trailer with 32 million views in a month, was bought by Riot Games, switched engines, was cancelled and went back to the people who created it.

This is the whole story, period by period, with the dates recorded by the official blog and the press. For a quick lookup, the timeline of numbers and milestones keeps just the dates.

Before Hytale (2011 to 2014)

The adventure maps

Simon Collins-Laflamme, known in Minecraft as "hypixel", and Philippe Touchette, aka Rezzus, started out making adventure maps. Let's plays ruled YouTube at the time, and the duo's maps had boss fights and redstone contraptions. In April 2020, Simon wrote that it had been nine years since the two of them launched Hypixel. That puts the start of the brand in 2011.

In a 2018 interview with Rock Paper Shotgun, Simon described the problem that changed everything. Players knew how to install the maps, but they didn't know how to find their friends to play. The answer was to open a server.

The Hypixel server

The Hypixel server opened in April 2013. Players showed up by the thousands, and the team started betting on minigames. They were short matches to kill time while friends logged in, like The Walls, Bed Wars and UHC. They ended up more popular than the rest of the server.

Hypixel collected four Guinness World Records. One is the most popular independent server for a videogame, with 64,533 concurrent players. Another is the most unique players logged into a Minecraft server, with 14.1 million.

The EULA wake-up call

In 2014, Mojang began enforcing the Minecraft license rules against selling gameplay advantages on servers. Aaron "Noxy" Donaghey, who would later run the studio, told Rock Paper Shotgun the episode was a reminder. Hypixel had a huge community, but it lived on someone else's playing field. The team wanted a field of its own.

There was a technical reason too. Turbo Kart Racer, a Mario Kart style racing minigame, struggled because the Minecraft camera couldn't pull back far enough, and players spun out without knowing why. With their own engine, limits like that would go away.

The secret years (2015 to 2018)

Development of Hytale began in 2015, funded by the server's success. The date is in the Hypixel Studios founding press release, published on December 13, 2018.

The game team split off from the server team. The official FAQ from December 2018 says this had happened "over two years" earlier. In 2016, according to the same founding press release, Riot Games met the team. The person saying so is Dylan Jadeja, then president of Riot. Remember that name.

Hypixel Studios was formally founded in 2018, separate from Hypixel Inc., which still runs the server. The money came from an investor group led by Riot, with names like Dennis "Thresh" Fong and designer Rob Pardo. At the announcement, the studio had more than 40 developers spread around the world.

The reveal (December 2018)

On Sunday, December 9, 2018, the Hytale website got a countdown, and the Hypixel community spent the week guessing what was coming. On December 13, the trailer dropped.

The internal goal was 250,000 views in the first week. One million was the best-case scenario. A month later, on January 13, 2019, the official blog counted 32 million. The team said it had backup plans for almost everything in the reveal, except a trailer this big. Job applications passed 900 in the first month, when the team expected a few dozen. As of September 2026, the video has more than 63 million views on the official channel.

The promise was huge. The FAQ published on reveal day laid out three pillars: an Adventure mode in a procedurally generated world, a minigames platform and tools for every kind of creator. The game would launch on PC and Mac. The date was left open.

The wait and Riot (2019 and 2020)

In May 2019, the studio explained that Hytale would be ready when it was ready, with no date for the beta. On November 27, 2019, came the first concrete target: the game would be playable by everyone in 2021.

On April 16, 2020, Simon and Noxy published letters to the community with the news: Riot Games was acquiring Hypixel Studios. The studio would stay independent, with Riot as parent company, a bigger budget and its first physical office, in Derry~Londonderry, Northern Ireland, Noxy's hometown. Simon stepped down as CEO to focus on his health, his family and the Hypixel server. Noxy, a former Rioter, took over.

In the same letter, Noxy explained why the studio had gone quiet for much of 2019. That was the time spent negotiating the partnership. The FAQ from that day kept the 2021 target. The Hypixel server was left out of the deal.

Delays and the new engine (2020 to 2025)

The 2021 target falls

On December 10, 2020, the blog admitted there was no launch plan. The pandemic had kept the team from working at full capacity. On July 1, 2021, came the harder message: Hytale would not launch before 2023, and it could take longer. In November 2021, the team said it was prototyping big changes to how the world of Orbis was structured and generated.

The switch to C++

On July 22, 2022, the studio confirmed what the community had spotted in job listings, which had stopped asking for Java and C#. Hytale's engine, client and server, would be rebuilt in C++, designed to run on many platforms. In the same post, the 2023 window was dropped.

The old engine, later nicknamed "legacy", used C# on the client and Java on the server. It was the engine behind the trailer and nearly every blog post. The new one had to catch up from scratch.

Three years on the new engine

On November 30, 2023, director John Hendricks said engine development had been brought fully in-house and that the first external playtest had taken place. The team still used the legacy engine to prototype. On July 29, 2024, the first images from the new engine came out.

On December 17, 2024, the studio celebrated the internal milestone nicknamed "Blonks": a hundred players at once in the Capital, something the legacy engine never handled. The March 28, 2025 update showed lighting and tree improvements and revealed an adult Kweebec in concept art. It was the last development update published under Riot.

The cancellation (June 23, 2025)

On June 23, 2025, Noxy announced the end of Hytale's development and the winding down of Hypixel Studios over the following months. In the message, he said nobody at Hypixel or at Riot wanted that outcome. After years of trying every path, it had become clear the game could not be delivered in a way that lived up to its promise. He pointed to a vision that changed over time, a bar that kept rising and technical ambitions that grew more complex, even after rebuilding the engine.

The trailer had 61 million views that day, according to GamesRadar. Six and a half years after the reveal, the game looked buried. The community answered with the #savehytale movement, which the studio later credited as decisive in bringing the founders back.

The buyback (June to November 2025)

The founder steps back in

A few days later, Simon said on X that he would reach out to Riot to try to buy the game back. He asked people to keep expectations "extremely low", since big companies almost never hand back cancelled projects. Rock Paper Shotgun reported it on June 30.

In July, Simon joked about spending 25 million dollars on the purchase. On August 12, he sent a final counter offer that, in his words, was worth ten times the game's market value. On August 13, he announced he was in active negotiations with Riot. He also said Riot no longer had a team and nobody there could even compile the game.

Hytale is saved

On November 17, 2025, the official blog came back with the headline "HYTALE IS SAVED!". Simon and Philippe Touchette had bought 100% of Hytale. The two committed to funding the game out of their own pockets for ten years, with no investors or publisher. More than 30 former developers of the project were rehired.

Riot confirmed on X that it had chosen among several offers. Dylan Jadeja, now Riot's CEO, the same person who met the team in 2016, said the decision put players first. Noxy thanked Simon publicly, but did not return to the project.

The technical decision was radical. There were two engines. The legacy one, in C# and Java and PC-only, had been quite advanced three or four years earlier. The new one, in C++, had about three years of work behind it and lagged far behind in gameplay. By the studio's estimate, it would need two more years before an Early Access. The C++ engine was dropped, and Hytale went back to the trailer build.

Eight weeks to launch

That same week, the studio released a 16-minute video of raw gameplay. On November 28, the date arrived: Early Access on January 13, 2026, with pre-purchase from December 13, 2025, exactly seven years after the trailer. The post said the team had merged more than 300 GitHub branches into one playable build and that the team had grown to 50 people. The most quoted line came at the end: anyone uncomfortable with pre-ordering should not buy.

On December 3, the first FAQ of the new era answered the biggest questions. The game would not launch on Steam at first, consoles would come later and there would be no mobile version. The editions cost 19.99 dollars (Standard), 35 (Supporter) and 70 (Cursebreaker Founder's Edition). On January 12, the day before launch, Simon announced that pre-purchases already covered the next two years of development.

The launch (January 13, 2026)

Hytale entered Early Access on January 13, 2026, with Exploration mode, Creative mode and modding support, on Windows, macOS and Linux. The launch post noted that everything was done in eight weeks, starting from a build more than four years old. Camera, movement, combat, crafting, building, audio and rendering were all reworked in that time.

On day one, Hytale became the most watched game on Twitch, with more than 420,000 viewers, according to PC Gamer. Simon expected more than 1 million players that day. The studio did not publish an official number. Fan sites reported 2.8 million, citing a livestream, but that figure never appeared in an official statement. The next day, Riot congratulated the team on X, and the Hytale account thanked Riot for helping bring the game home.

2026: a game under construction

January and February: putting out fires

Update 1 landed four days after launch, on January 17, with dinosaurs in the underground jungles and a large batch of fixes. Update 2, on January 24, reworked pickaxe progression and gave servers automatic updates.

Update 3, on February 17, brought taming, map markers and Brazilian Portuguese in the game. The notes also set the pace: weekly pre-releases bundled into a stable update every two to six weeks, and larger chapters to tell the Cursebreaker arc.

March to May: foundations

In March, the studio launched its first modding contest with CurseForge, with 100,000 dollars in prizes. By then, the game already had more than 5,000 mods and 20 million downloads on CurseForge. Update 4, on March 26, brought more than 500 blocks, proximity voice chat and the emote wheel.

On April 28, server owners got the tools to list their servers on the official directory. On May 11, the contest winners were announced, picked from almost 400 approved entries. Update 5, on May 26, added controller support, a friends list with Discord, the in-game server browser and the Trigger Volume, which builds events without code.

June to August: open source and Chapter 1 in sight

On June 22, Hypixel announced Hytale Shared Source on X. It covers the full server source code, the network protocol and every game asset, including content that never shipped, on GitHub. Access follows the Unreal Engine model: own the game and accept the terms. Pull requests are not accepted yet.

On July 16, Simon published the first look at Chapter 1. The focus is the goblin faction, with the first handcrafted dungeon, the first multi-phase boss, the Goblin Breach event, a rune-based ability system, gadgets and even boats. The post also showed the party minigames, with 14 micro-games, and admitted bluntly that there were still few reasons to come back and play at that moment.

Update 6, on August 27, delivered Hardcore mode, spectator mode, the in-game mod browser and friend connections without a VPN. It was the first cycle with community code accepted into the game, and one of the contributors, MelodicAlbuild, was hired.

September: Update 7 in pre-release

Update 7 has been in pre-release since September 3, with notes in weekly parts: September 3, 10 and 17. It is the first batch of Chapter 1 content, with six reworked goblins, including the Goblin Feastmaster, the first five combat runes and the goblin gadgets. As of this writing, the studio had not announced a date for Chapter 1.

Where the story stands today

Eleven years after the project began, Hytale is in players' hands. It gets pre-releases every week, its server code is open to license holders and it is made by a team that numbered around 50 people on the eve of launch. The game remains in Early Access, and the studio has said since the buyback that this phase should last at least a few years, with no rush to 1.0.

Dates and numbers that don't match

  • Start of the project. The founding press release says 2015. The December 2018 FAQ says the team split from the server team "over two years" earlier, which points to 2016. The project may have started before the teams split, but no official source spells it out.
  • Origin of Hypixel. The founding press release says the server opened in April 2013. In 2020, Simon spoke of nine years of Hypixel, counting from the map-making days, around 2011.
  • Trailer's first month. The official blog recorded 32 million on January 13, 2019. Some outlets rounded it to "over 30 million" or "31 million".
  • Views over time. GamesRadar cited 61 million at the cancellation, in June 2025. PC Gamer, 62 million at launch. The YouTube counter was past 63 million in September 2026.
  • Rehired team. On November 17, 2025, it was "over 30". On November 28, "more than 40", with 50 people in total. The number grew between the two posts.
  • Modding contest. PocketGamer.biz reported it on March 3, 2026. The official blog post is dated March 5.
  • Launch players. The only figure tied to the studio is Simon's expectation of more than 1 million on day one. Bigger numbers circulated without official confirmation.
