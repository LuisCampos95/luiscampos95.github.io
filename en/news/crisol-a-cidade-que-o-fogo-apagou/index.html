Crucible: the city the fire erased

The game says Cindercloth came from the bones of a Crucible citizen. The rise of the city of fire artisans, the messenger who arrived before the end and the cursed flame that wiped it off the map: the gateway to our lore series.

Every time someone puts on a Cindercloth Tunic, they're wearing a dead man's clothes. That's not a figure of speech: the game itself says Cindercloth was salvaged from the charred bones of a former citizen of Crucible. A city nobody visits, that isn't on the map, and that is still scattered across all of Zone 4: in the ash, in the skeletons and in the metal we mine.

This is the gateway to our Hytale lore series. Quotes come straight from the game. Anything that's our own guess is marked as Theory:.

The city that worked fire

Before it turned to ash, Crucible was rich. The Adamantite Ingot remembers:

"This rare metal was once instrumental to the advancements made by the peoples of Crucible. Though little remains of the city's former splendor, traces of the resilient ore can still be found in the depths beneath those scorched lands."

Notice the plural: the peoples of Crucible. It wasn't a single-race village. It was a place where different folk lived together and thrived on a metal you now only find by digging deep.

The engine of that city was fire. The Essence of Fire says:

"This energy once gave form to the dreams of Crucible's artisans. Today it merely lingers in the creatures that dwell amid the desolated volcanic regions."

Artisans shaping dreams out of fire. Even the name fits: a crucible is the vessel where metal melts to become something else. A whole city named after a forge.

Even a throwaway item, the Crude Torch, sums up the tragedy:

"For the last peoples of Orbis, fire was both a source of creativity that elevated their civilization to new heights, and the force that would ultimately lead to their undoing."

That civilization left behind a whole furniture line called "Lost Civilization": bench, bed, bookshelf, brazier, banner, coffin. And a single statue with a proper name, the Bearer of Fire Statue. Even the Bandage remembers "the knights of a lost civilization", who patched themselves up with whatever they had on the battlefield.

Theory: the Bearer of Fire was the most important figure in Crucible. A founder, a saint, or the first to master the flame. No other piece of that civilization got a person's name.

A messenger, a Councilor and the Scaraks

The only text that shows Crucible still alive arrived in the 09/24 pre-release. It's an Enkindled journal, the Old Enkindled Journal: Vermin, written by someone who can't stand a noise coming from underground anymore:

"I hear them down there. Every. Day. The clanking doesn't stop. What are they? Rats? They have to be bigger than that. I hope it's not spiders. The Guardians were meant to go down there but they've been in disarray since a messenger arrived from Crucible yesterday. Something about a Councilor and the Scaraks - I didn't quite hear it. I just hope they do something about it soon."

It's the most human scene in the whole lore. An ordinary person complaining about noise under the floor, with no idea they're writing in the last days of their world. The Guardians are still alive and on duty, Crucible still sends messengers, and a single piece of news about a Councilor and the Scaraks is enough to throw the whole guard into disarray.

The Scaraks fell too, and the game leaves a grim hint on the Statue of the Founding Builder: the stone feels stained by "its silent witness of the atrocities committed under the first and last Scarak Empress".

Theory: Crucible was ruled by a council, and the message was a warning of war or betrayal involving the Scaraks. And the clanking down below? Could be vermin. Or someone building something they shouldn't.

The cursed flame

After the journal, silence. The next time Crucible shows up, it's already gone. The Cindercloth Scraps tells the ending in two sentences:

"When the cursed flame scoured Crucible it left nothing unchanged. Even these scraps of fabric - salvaged from the charred bones of a former denizen - continue to emit an uncanny heat."

Nothing unchanged. The game doesn't say fire, it says a cursed flame, one that won't quite go out: the scraps are still warm in your hand today.

And it wasn't just the city. The Enkindled Sigil speaks "of a civilization now lost to a calamity that left Orbis scarred". The whole world carries the scar.

The Zone 4 soundtrack seems to have read the script. Some tracks: "Calamity", "Tragedy", "Lost Brethren", "March of the Turned", "The Cost of Survival" and "Whispering Scars".

Theory: the cursed flame came out of Crucible's own workshops. The Enkindled had a rite that "set people alight", and a charred letter talks about building "it" for war, against the advice of a wise old sage. That part lives in The rite that set people alight.

The dead that still burn

Anyone who walks the Cinder Wastes has already met Crucible's people without knowing it. The Burnt Skeletons come as knight, soldier, lancer, archer, gunner, praetorian, wizard and alchemist. That's not a pack of monsters, it's the army and guilds of a big city, still in formation. And almost all of them drop Cindercloth Scraps.

Some haven't even cooled down. Incandescent Skeletons glow like embers, and the Incandescent Skeleton Head is just that: a head that refuses to go out.

And someone keeps calling those dead back. The Burnt Skeleton Praetorian sometimes carries the Grimoire: Rekindle Embers, a grimoire that "raises skeletal minions from nearby bone piles". In a city with more bones left than stone, that spell never runs out of material.

Above them all stand the Revenant Guardian. Their Memory says they helped win the Great War, "though even they were powerless in the face of what followed". What followed is the subject of What came after the victory.

Theory: the Burnt Skeletons are Crucible's own population, caught by the flame mid-shift. The soldier still patrols, the alchemist still roams the sewers, the praetorian still guards someone who no longer exists.

Those who escaped the flame

Not everyone was in Crucible when it burned. The Essence of Ice speaks of a group that left before:

"When the Outlanders rejected the flame and split from their brethren, they turned instead toward the colder regions and the fragile strength found in the advancing sheets of ice."

The Outlanders, today enemies of almost everyone in Orbis, were once part of the same people and said no to the flame. The split and the war that followed are in Those who refused the flame.

And some want the flame back. The goblins live in the ruins of an Enkindled settlement and spend their lives imitating a civilization they only know from legend. The Goblin Lobber treats every bomb as a rehearsal, hoping its blast "will be as grand as the one spoken of in legends". Their obsession is in Goblins think they inherited a kingdom.

Theory: the "great flame" of goblin legend is the cursed flame. They dream of repeating exactly what destroyed Crucible.

What's left

Crucible isn't on the map, but its remains are. Zone 4 has two regions named like a burial: the Cinder Wastes and the Charred Woodlands. In the Wastes, the game scatters ruined cities with a colosseum, library, market, bank, temple, smithy, villa and even street lamps. A place with a bank and a colosseum is no village.

Beneath those ruins runs a sewer, and it even has its own track on the Zone 4 soundtrack, "Sewers". That's where the Burnt Skeleton Soldier and the Burnt Skeleton Alchemist show up. The Wastes' caves also hold layouts of an underground city, with a central square, gates, walls and towers, which the team first drew by hand, on paper.

Deeper still, the Fragment: Orbis - Tal'Kesi Refuge speaks of a place that survived it all: "Deep beneath the desolate wastelands left by an ancient civilization, a primordial haven thrives."

And a detail for the file diggers: the game already has a weather setup named after the city, Crucible, ready and unused anywhere in the current world.

Theory: the ruins of the Cinder Wastes are Crucible's outskirts, not the city itself. The center, with the council and the Bearer of Fire statue still standing, is being saved for a future update. And look at that Fragment again: it doesn't blame the flame for the wastelands, it says a civilization left them behind.

Open questions

  • What was making that clanking underground, every single day?
  • Did the Councilor betray Crucible or try to save it?
  • What did the Scaraks have to do with the message that broke the Guardians?
  • Was the cursed flame an accident, a weapon or a punishment?
  • Who was the Bearer of Fire, and why is theirs the only statue with a name?
  • Why are Crucible's dead still warm today?
  • If the goblins find the "great flame", does Orbis burn again?
