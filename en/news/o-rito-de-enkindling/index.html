The rite that set people alight

One pre-release journal line mentions the Rite of Enkindling. What it seems to have been, what the flame gave those who went through it and how the war may have turned a rite into the cursed flame.

The Rite of Enkindling shows up exactly once in all of Hytale, on the last line of a journal. The writer is on watch, far from everyone, and regrets missing something:

Old Enkindled Journal: The Rite

"Ah, how I wanted to be there to witness her Rite of Enkindling."

It's not much, but it's the piece that explains the name of an entire people. What the game says is in quotes. Our guesses are marked Theory:.

What one line tells us

  • The rite belongs to one person. "Her rite", not "the rite". It's not a holiday, it's a moment in someone's life.
  • It's meant to be witnessed. The writer wanted "to be there to witness" it, the way you'd talk about a graduation or a wedding.
  • It happened in wartime, somewhere else. The same journal opens with "maybe I should have gone with them" and explains almost everyone had left to hold the line against the Outlanders. The rite went where the war was.

Theory: the Rite of Enkindling took place at the front, or because of it. Someone had to be set alight before they fought.

Enkindled means set alight

To *enkindle* is to light, to set aflame. The game's Portuguese localization followed the same idea and calls this people's metal blocks "Burning Iron", like the Enkindled Iron.

Theory: Enkindled wasn't just the name of a people, it was what a person became after the rite. You were born there, but only became Enkindled when you received the flame. That would explain why refusing the flame was enough for the Outlanders to stop being part of that people.

What the flame gave

The game never describes the rite, but it says a lot about the fire of that age.

Crude Torch

"For the last peoples of Orbis, fire was both a source of creativity that elevated their civilization to new heights, and the force that would ultimately lead to their undoing."

Not cooking fire. Creative power, the same energy that gave form to the dreams of Crucible's artisans, according to the Essence of Fire.

And here comes a new pre-release text, on the Runebinder's Lattice, the rune bench:

"A civilization once sought to claim the powers of an Avatar as their own. Had the Runebinder's way proved successful, perhaps it would have kept them from walking much darker paths."

The Arcanist's Workbench points the same way: the arcane arts are the closest "mere mortals" have come to Avatar powers.

Theory: the Rite of Enkindling was one of those darker paths. A civilization wanted an Avatar's power, the runes couldn't deliver it, and the flame could. Every person set alight in the rite carried a piece of a power that wasn't theirs.

What it cost

The Bone Fragments carries a warning that reads as if it were written for this people:

"These bone fragments - and the cursed creature from which they were salvaged - should serve as a reminder that some paths are better left untrodden."

Darker paths on the rune bench, paths better left untrodden on the bones. Both texts use the same image, and neither says which path it was.

From rite to workshop

The last piece is the charred Workshops of War letter.

Enkindled Letter: Workshops of War

"Magic is pure. Add mechanism to magic and the magic is tainted. [...] Yet now they say we should build it for war."

Read next to the journal, the letter hits harder. In peacetime, the flame passed from person to person, in a rite worth the trip to witness. Then the war tightened, and someone wanted to put that flame inside a machine.

Theory: the cursed flame that scoured Crucible is the flame of the rite after it went through the mechanism, exactly as the old sage warned. The Enkindled iron pipes and chimneys would be what's left of that turn. None of this is confirmed, but the letter picks its word well: the magic doesn't break, it gets tainted.

Open questions

  • Who was "her", and did she survive the rite?
  • Did every Enkindled go through the rite, or only a chosen few?
  • Where did the flame come from: Gaia, an Avatar, or something the Enkindled built themselves?
  • Was the Runebinder a person, another people or another age?
